Researcher-controlled speaker mapping

Speaker-count workflow · technical rationale

Review the voices first. Confirm the participant count second.

A fixed number can constrain clustering, but it cannot tell the system which voice belongs to a student, a teacher or an incidental speaker. The safer workflow makes every candidate voice audible and reviewable before the research timeline is constructed.

Recommended design Candidate detection → researcher mapping → dynamic N+3 output

The system still automates the full recording. Human input is limited to the methodological decision AI cannot make reliably: who belongs in the study.

04 · Claim boundary

What this design promises

The stronger method is not “more AI.” It is a clearer boundary between acoustic automation and research judgment.

The system can

  • Detect candidate acoustic speakers across the full recording.
  • Present representative evidence without requiring full-file listening.
  • Preserve excluded voices, merges and uncertain intervals in the audit trail.
  • Generate listening tracks and dynamic N+3 tiers after participant confirmation.

The system cannot infer by diarization alone

  • Whether a voice is a student, teacher or incidental participant.
  • Whether a requested fixed number matches the voices actually present.
  • Whether a short teacher intervention should count as a research turn.
  • Whether a muted-mirror track is equivalent to clean source separation.
Method principle
The system does not guess who belongs in the study. It makes the evidence reviewable, records the researcher’s decision, and only then constructs the research timeline.

This keeps teacher interventions visible without allowing them to silently corrupt participant attribution, floor states or transition metrics.
